EMPLOYEE SEPARATION AND EXIT POLICY

1. RESIGNATION PROCESS

Employees who wish to leave the organization must submit their resignation through the official HR portal. The resignation request must include the proposed last working date and the reason for separation. Once the resignation is successfully submitted, the reporting manager will receive a notification for review. Employees should not consider their resignation approved until the request has been reviewed and acknowledged by the appropriate manager and HR team.

2. NOTICE PERIOD

The standard notice period for permanent employees is 90 calendar days unless a different notice period is specifically mentioned in the employee's employment contract. The notice period starts from the date on which the resignation is successfully submitted through the HR portal. Employees are expected to continue performing their regular responsibilities during the notice period and must complete all assigned activities before their last working day.

Employees may request an early release from the standard notice period. Such requests must be approved by the reporting manager and HR. Approval of an early release is not automatic and depends on business requirements, project transition requirements, and successful completion of knowledge transfer activities.

3. KNOWLEDGE TRANSFER

Before the last working day, employees must complete the required knowledge transfer activities. This may include preparing technical documentation, conducting walkthrough sessions, transferring project responsibilities, and providing details of ongoing activities to the replacement employee or project team. The reporting manager is responsible for confirming that the required knowledge transfer has been completed.

4. HR CLEARANCE

Employees must complete all applicable HR clearance activities before their separation can be finalized. HR clearance may include confirmation of attendance records, leave balances, employee information, company assets, and other administrative requirements. Any pending clearance activity may delay the completion of the final separation process.

5. IT AND ASSET CLEARANCE

All company-owned assets must be returned before the employee's last working day unless an exception has been formally approved. Assets may include laptops, monitors, access cards, mobile devices, security tokens, and other equipment provided by the organization. The IT team will verify the returned assets and update the clearance status in the HR system.

Employees must also ensure that company data is not copied to personal devices, personal cloud storage, removable drives, or other unauthorized locations. Access to company applications and systems may be disabled after the employee's last working day.

6. FULL AND FINAL SETTLEMENT

The Full and Final Settlement (FFS) process begins after the required separation and clearance activities have been completed. The settlement may include eligible salary payments, leave encashment, reimbursements, deductions, recoveries, and other applicable components according to company policy and the employee's employment terms.

The final settlement amount may be affected by outstanding company asset recoveries, salary advances, loans, or other pending financial obligations. Employees should ensure that all required information and documents are submitted to HR to avoid unnecessary delays.

7. EXPERIENCE AND RELIEVING DOCUMENTS

After successful completion of the separation process and applicable clearance activities, the organization may issue experience and relieving documentation according to the applicable process. Employees should ensure that their personal contact information and mailing address are updated in the HR portal before separation.

8. EXIT INTERVIEW

Employees may be requested to participate in an exit interview before their last working day. The purpose of the exit interview is to collect feedback about the employee's experience, work environment, management practices, and other aspects of employment. Participation requirements may vary depending on organizational or business-unit processes.

9. RE-EMPLOYMENT

Former employees may be considered for future employment subject to applicable company rules, business requirements, and the employee's previous separation status. Re-employment is not guaranteed and will be evaluated according to the organization's recruitment and eligibility processes.

10. POLICY EXCEPTIONS

Any exception to this policy must receive appropriate approval from the relevant HR authority and business management. Verbal approvals should not be considered sufficient unless they are subsequently documented through the official organizational process.

This policy is intended to provide general guidance for employee separation. Specific employment contracts, applicable laws, organizational procedures, and formally approved exceptions may take precedence where applicable.
